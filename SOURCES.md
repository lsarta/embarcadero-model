## Market data behind the assumptions

**Rents.** SF citywide average asking rent was $70.31/SF annual gross in Q2 2026, up from $69.22 in Q1 (Cushman & Wakefield SF Office MarketBeat, Q2 2026). CBD Class A asking $76.60/SF; Class A Tier 1 direct asking $106.97/SF at 8.2% direct vacancy (same). CBRE's Q1 2026 Figures put average direct asking at $71.19 FSG, +3.7% year-over-year. Newmark's Q1 2026 report put direct asking at $67.77/SF, +0.3% quarter-over-quarter, and noted rents have "hovered around $68 per square foot for the past six quarters."

**Rent growth.** The band is deliberately wide: CBRE shows +3.7% YoY asking growth, but Newmark's six-quarters-flat observation means zero growth is a live scenario, not a stress case. Hence 0–4% rather than a tidier band.

**Vacancy and absorption.** Overall SF vacancy 30.1% at Q2 2026, down 360bps year-over-year, with five consecutive quarters of positive absorption (C&W Q2 2026). Kidder Mathews Q1 2026: 28.0% overall vacancy, 855K SF net absorption, strongest since 2019. The bifurcation matters more than the average: Class A Tier 1 direct vacancy is 8.2% (C&W) — the best assets are ~92% occupied while the citywide average carries the distressed tail. That bifurcation is why stabilized occupancy of 92% is defensible for an EC tower despite a 30% citywide vacancy rate.

**Sales comps.** Q1 2026: five major SF office investment deals totaling $1.27B across 2.45M SF; the Transamerica Pyramid complex traded at $922/SF, the highest since 2022 (CBRE Q1 2026 Figures). 101 California reportedly seeking ~$900/SF (SF Standard, Jan 2026). At the other end: 550 Kearny listed at low-to-mid $300s/SF (28.6% occupied, value-add), 455 Market guidance in the upper $300s/SF (The Real Deal, May 2026). 2024 market-wide average was ~$310/SF (CBRE, via SF Examiner) — depressed by the distressed mix. Our $494/SF for an 85%-leased Class A tower sits between the reset trades and trophy pricing, which is a judgment, not a comp.

**Cap rates.** Listed SF commercial cap rates average 6.08% (LoopNet) to 6.29% (CityFeet) across all asset types, 2026. CBRE's 2026 U.S. Real Estate Market Outlook expects cap rates to compress 5–15bps for most property types, with quality assets compressing more. Office should price wide of a blended all-asset average, so the exit band extends to 7.00% on the high side. No office-specific SF CBD cap rate print was available in public sources — that's a real gap, and it's why the exit cap is labeled judgment.

**Operating expenses.** The weakest-sourced input in the model, and it ranks near the top of the sensitivity table — a combination worth noticing. BOMA's last widely published SF figure was $11.85/SF (2018 Office EER), which excludes property taxes and predates significant insurance and utility escalation. Prop 13 means the tax basis resets at sale: roughly 1.18% of a $390M basis is ~$5.80/SF alone. Mid-$20s all-in gross opex for a Class A high-rise is our estimate on top of dated data. If you have access to current BOMA EER data or actual SF tower operating statements, this is the first number to replace.

**Capital reserves.** $0.35/SF is a typical lender minimum and is almost certainly understated for towers of this vintage (Embarcadero Center was built 1971–1982). Real capex for elevators, systems, and lobby/amenity competitiveness would be a multiple of this. The model has no TI/LC line at all — leasing the building from 85% to 92% is currently modeled as free, which it is not. Both are honest gaps; both are good afternoon projects.
